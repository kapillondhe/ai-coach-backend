# Shoulder-Safe Exercise Substitutions

## General principle

Most non-acute shoulder pain during pressing or overhead work is aggravated by
positions combining internal rotation with load at end-range overhead motion (a
common "impingement" pattern). Substitutions generally aim to press in a plane the
shoulder tolerates better, or reduce range of motion at the aggravating end position.
Sudden loss of strength, numbness, or suspected labral/rotator-cuff tear warrants
medical evaluation before continuing to load the joint.

## Overhead press alternatives

- **Barbell overhead press → Landmine press**: the angled bar path keeps the arm more
  in front of the body rather than directly overhead, reducing impingement-provoking
  positions.
- **Barbell overhead press → Neutral-grip dumbbell press**: a neutral (palms-facing)
  grip keeps the shoulder in a more externally rotated position through the lift,
  which many people with shoulder pain tolerate better than a pronated barbell grip.
- **Behind-the-neck press → Avoid**: this variant places the shoulder in a
  particularly provocative position and is rarely worth the risk versus a front press.

## Bench/chest press alternatives

- **Flat barbell bench press → Dumbbell bench press**: dumbbells let each arm find its
  own comfortable path and don't lock the shoulder into a fixed bar position.
- **Flat bench press → Floor press**: the floor limits range of motion at the bottom,
  removing the deepest (often most aggravating) part of the movement.
- **Dips → Push-up with hands on slightly raised support**: reduces the degree of
  shoulder extension at the bottom position compared to a full dip.

## Accessory/rehab work

Rotator-cuff and scapular-stability work (band external rotations, face pulls,
scapular wall slides) alongside — not instead of — the substituted pressing movement
is commonly recommended to address the underlying weakness that often contributes to
shoulder impingement patterns.
