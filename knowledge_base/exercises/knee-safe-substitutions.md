# Knee-Safe Exercise Substitutions

## General principle

For most non-acute knee discomfort (not a diagnosed ligament or meniscus tear
requiring medical clearance), the goal is to reduce shear and compressive load on the
knee joint while keeping the surrounding muscles — quads, hamstrings, glutes — working,
since weakness around the joint often makes knee pain worse over time. Anyone with
acute swelling, instability, or a suspected structural injury should see a medical
professional before continuing to train the joint.

## Squat and lunge alternatives

- **Back/front squat → Leg press**: reduces the stabilization demand and shear force
  on the knee by fixing the bar path, while still loading quads and glutes heavily.
- **Back/front squat → Box squat to a higher box**: shortens range of motion at the
  bottom, reducing peak knee flexion where pain is often worst.
- **Walking lunge → Reverse lunge**: stepping backward instead of forward reduces the
  forward knee translation that aggravates many anterior knee issues.
- **Bulgarian split squat → Step-up (low box)**: keeps single-leg quad and glute work
  without the deep rear-leg stretch position.

## Direct quad/hamstring work with less knee load

- **Leg extension → Isometric wall sit** (moderate angle, ~60-90 degrees) or
  **Spanish squat**: builds quad strength with a controlled, low-shear joint angle.
- **Running/plyometrics → Cycling, swimming, or rowing**: maintains cardiovascular
  and lower-body conditioning with far lower peak knee impact forces.

## Progression note

When returning to full squats/lunges after knee discomfort resolves, progress range
of motion and load gradually rather than returning to the previous working weight
immediately — a sharp jump in either is a common cause of re-aggravation.
