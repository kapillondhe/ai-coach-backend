# Pace-Based Training Zones for Swimming (Critical Swim Speed)

## Why swimming uses pace rather than heart rate

Heart rate runs meaningfully lower in swimming than in running or cycling at an
equivalent effort — the horizontal body position, water pressure on the torso, and
constant cooling all suppress heart rate by roughly 10-20 beats/minute relative to
land-based max heart rate at the same perceived exertion. Applying running- or
cycling-derived heart-rate zones (see `heart-rate-training-zones.md`,
`power-based-training-zones.md`) to swimming under-targets every zone. Structured
swim training is instead built around pace per 100m anchored to Critical Swim Speed
(CSS), the swimming equivalent of lactate/functional threshold.

## Testing Critical Swim Speed

CSS is calculated from a 400m and 200m time trial, swum on separate, well-rested
efforts (typically in the same session with adequate recovery between, or on
separate days for a more reliable result):

- CSS pace per 100m = (400m time − 200m time) ÷ 2, expressed as seconds per 100m.
- The test approximates the pace sustainable through aerobic/anaerobic metabolism
  balance over roughly 15-30 minutes, functioning as a proxy for lactate threshold
  without needing blood lactate testing.
- Retest every 6-8 weeks during a structured block, the same cadence recommended
  for cycling FTP in `power-based-training-zones.md` — a stale CSS misdirects every
  zone-based set that follows from it.

## Zone definitions (relative to CSS pace per 100m)

- **Zone 1 — Recovery (CSS + 15-20+ sec/100m)**: very easy swimming used for
  warm-up, cool-down, and active recovery between hard sets.
- **Zone 2 — Aerobic base (CSS + 8-15 sec/100m)**: the aerobic-base workhorse pace
  where most weekly volume belongs, directly analogous to Zone 2 in the
  heart-rate/power models — fully sustainable, technique-focused swimming.
- **Zone 3 — Aerobic threshold (CSS + 3-7 sec/100m)**: moderately hard, sustainable
  for longer aerobic sets (e.g. broken 200s-400s); useful but, as with the
  land-based "gray zone," easy to overuse as a substitute for genuine easy
  swimming.
- **Zone 4 — CSS/threshold (CSS pace, ± 2 sec/100m)**: effort at or near CSS
  itself, held for repeated middle-distance intervals (e.g. 6-10 x 100m) with
  short rest — the primary zone for raising CSS directly.
- **Zone 5 — VO2max (CSS − 3-8 sec/100m)**: short, hard repeats (25-100m) with
  generous rest, raising aerobic ceiling rather than threshold.
- **Zone 6 — Sprint/neuromuscular (maximal, brief)**: all-out efforts of 15-25m or
  a few strokes, limited by neuromuscular power rather than aerobic capacity, used
  for stroke-rate and starts/turns work.

## Programming zones across a training week

- The same polarized pattern used in `heart-rate-training-zones.md` and
  `power-based-training-zones.md` applies: the large majority of weekly volume in
  Zone 1-2, with 1-2 dedicated CSS/threshold or VO2max sessions and the rest kept
  genuinely easy.
- Swimming is unusually technique-limited relative to running or cycling (see
  `swim-training-plan.md`), so a poor-technique swimmer pushed into Zone 4-5 sets
  before technique is established tends to reinforce inefficient stroke mechanics
  under fatigue rather than building useful fitness — technique work at easy pace
  should take priority early in a training block.
- Rest intervals matter as much as pace in swim set design: shortening rest at a
  fixed pace effectively raises the training stimulus without changing the target
  zone, and is a common way swim sets progress week to week within the same
  nominal pace target.

## Stroke rate and distance-per-stroke as a secondary signal

Alongside pace, tracking stroke rate (strokes/minute) and distance per stroke
(distance ÷ strokes) helps distinguish a pace change driven by genuine fitness from
one driven by a breakdown in technique under fatigue — a swimmer holding CSS pace
only by sharply increasing stroke rate and shortening each stroke is usually
fatiguing technique rather than demonstrating fitness, and this pattern is worth
flagging even when the pace target on the clock is being met.
