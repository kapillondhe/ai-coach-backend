# Heart-Rate Zones and Easy-Running Intensity Distribution

## Why most marathon training should be easy

Elite and well-trained sub-elite endurance athletes typically run 75-85% of total
training volume at easy, low-intensity effort and reserve only 15-25% for
moderate-to-hard efforts (a pattern often called "polarized" or "80/20" training).
Runners who train too much of their volume at a moderate ("gray zone") effort —
harder than truly easy but not hard enough to be a quality session — tend to
accumulate fatigue without a proportional fitness return, and are at higher injury
risk than runners following an easy/hard distribution at the same total volume.

## Zone definitions (5-zone model, % of max heart rate)

- **Zone 1 (50-60%)**: recovery effort, barely elevated over resting.
- **Zone 2 (60-70%)**: easy/aerobic-base effort — fully conversational, "easy" runs
  and most long-run miles belong here.
- **Zone 3 (70-80%)**: moderate/"tempo-adjacent" effort — sustainable for 30-60
  minutes, comfortably hard, limited conversation.
- **Zone 4 (80-90%)**: threshold/tempo effort — sustainable for roughly 20-60
  minutes, corresponds closely to lactate threshold and, for many trained runners,
  somewhere near half-marathon effort.
- **Zone 5 (90-100%)**: VO2max/interval effort — sustainable only for a few minutes
  at a time, used in short repeats.

Max heart rate estimated by age-based formulas (220 minus age) carries substantial
individual error (±10-12 bpm is common); a measured max from a hard effort test or
race is more reliable when precision matters.

## Applying zones to a marathon plan

- **Easy runs and most of the long run**: Zone 2. The most common training mistake
  in recreational runners is running "easy" days meaningfully faster than Zone 2,
  which blunts the aerobic adaptations easy running is meant to build and adds
  fatigue that compromises quality sessions.
- **Marathon-pace segments**: typically sit around the Zone 3/4 boundary depending
  on fitness — marathon race effort for most trained runners is sustainable for
  2-5 hours, below true lactate threshold.
- **Tempo runs**: Zone 4, developing the ability to sustain a hard-but-controlled
  effort — directly relevant to holding goal marathon pace late in the race.
- **Interval sessions**: Zone 5, used sparingly (typically once per week or less)
  during the build phase to raise aerobic ceiling; overusing high-intensity work
  crowds out the easy volume that builds the aerobic base a marathon depends on.

## Pace vs. heart rate as the primary guide

Heart rate lags effort by tens of seconds to a couple of minutes and is affected by
heat, dehydration, caffeine, and accumulated fatigue (cardiac drift), so it can read
higher than usual for a genuinely easy effort under those conditions. Runners newer
to heart-rate training sometimes chase a heart-rate number rather than a true easy
effort; when heart rate and perceived effort disagree, trust perceived effort
(conversational pace) for easy runs and use heart-rate data mainly to catch
drift over multi-week blocks rather than to micromanage a single run.
