# Marathon Fueling: Carb Loading, Race-Day, and Hydration

## Carbohydrate loading (final 1-3 days)

Increasing carbohydrate intake to roughly 8-12 g/kg/day for the 24-48 hours before
the race, while tapering training volume to near zero, maximizes muscle glycogen
stores beyond what normal training-level carb intake provides. This does not require
the older "depletion" phase (deliberately depleting glycogen with a low-carb period
before loading) — modern carb loading without prior depletion achieves comparable
glycogen supercompensation with far less disruption to training and mood. Fat and
fiber intake should drop somewhat during this window to make room for the added
carbohydrate without causing GI distress, and fiber specifically should be reduced
in the final 24 hours to lower the risk of race-morning GI issues.

## In-race carbohydrate intake

Trained endurance athletes can absorb roughly 60-90 g of carbohydrate per hour from
a mix of glucose and fructose sources (multiple-transportable-carb formulations, as
in most commercial gels/chews/drinks), higher than the ~60 g/hour ceiling of
glucose-only sources. Guidelines for marathon-distance efforts (roughly 2.5+ hours):

- Begin fueling early (15-20 minutes in) rather than waiting until feeling depleted —
  once glycogen debt and GI distress set in, they're hard to fully reverse mid-race.
- Target 30-60 g carbohydrate/hour for most recreational marathoners; runners with a
  trained gut and finish times under ~3 hours can push toward 60-90 g/hour for a
  further performance benefit.
- Practice the exact race-day fueling plan (product, timing, quantity) in training
  long runs beforehand — race day is not the time to try a new gel or drink mix, since
  GI tolerance to concentrated carbohydrate is trainable and individual.

## Hydration and electrolytes

Drink to thirst rather than a fixed volume schedule for most recreational runners;
both under-drinking (dehydration, heat-related performance loss) and over-drinking
(hyponatremia, a dangerous dilution of blood sodium) impair performance, and
over-drinking beyond thirst is the more dangerous of the two failure modes. Sodium
losses through sweat vary widely between individuals (roughly 200-2000+ mg/liter of
sweat); "salty sweaters" (visible salt residue on skin/clothing after long runs)
benefit from an electrolyte-containing drink or supplement rather than water alone,
especially in hot/humid conditions or races lasting 3+ hours.

## Pre-race meal (morning of)

A pre-race breakfast of 1-4 g/kg carbohydrate, low in fat and fiber, eaten 2-4 hours
before the start allows digestion to mostly complete before running while topping off
liver glycogen depleted overnight. Familiar, previously-tested foods only — this is
not the time to try a new pre-race meal.
