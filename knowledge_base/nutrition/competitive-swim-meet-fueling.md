# Competitive Swim Meet Fueling

## How swim meet fueling differs from single-event endurance racing

Unlike a marathon or triathlon (see `marathon-fueling.md`,
`triathlon-race-fueling.md`), most competitive swim events themselves are
short — seconds to a few minutes — so in-race fueling during an individual
event is not a consideration the way it is for hour-plus efforts. The fueling
challenge in swimming is instead the meet structure: swimmers are often on-site
for many hours across a single day or multiple days, with warm-up swimming,
long gaps between individual events or heats, and repeated short, high-intensity
efforts that each draw on glycogen and require some recovery before the next.

## Pre-meet and pre-event fueling

- A carbohydrate-focused pre-meet breakfast (1-4 g/kg, low in fat and fiber),
  eaten 2-4 hours before warm-ups begin, follows the same digestion-window
  logic as `marathon-fueling.md` — topping off liver glycogen depleted
  overnight without leaving a heavy meal to digest during a warm-up swim.
- Before an individual event later in the day, a small, familiar, fast-digesting
  carbohydrate snack (e.g. a banana, a small portion of a sports drink or gel)
  30-60 minutes prior tops up blood glucose without the GI load of a full meal
  — swimmers racing multiple events across a day should favor several small
  snacks over large meals timed around the schedule.

## Between-event fueling across a multi-hour or multi-day meet

- Recovery nutrition (carbohydrate plus a moderate amount of protein) in the
  30-60 minutes after each event or session, when the gap before the next
  swim allows, supports glycogen replenishment for the next effort — the same
  window emphasized in `protein-timing.md`, applied repeatedly across a meet
  day rather than once.
- Multi-day meets place a premium on evening recovery meals being
  carbohydrate-adequate, not just protein-focused, since starting the next
  day's session with incompletely restored glycogen compounds fatigue across
  the meet — directly analogous to the multi-day stage-race guidance in
  `cycling-endurance-fueling.md`.
- Long gaps between heats/finals (sometimes several hours) tempt swimmers to
  either overeat from boredom/nerves or under-eat from pre-race anxiety;
  planning small, familiar snacks in advance for these gaps avoids both
  failure modes.

## Hydration

Indoor pool environments are warm and humid, and immersion in water masks the
sensation of sweating, both of which make swimmers prone to underestimating
fluid losses relative to a runner or cyclist in the same conditions — despite
feeling less overtly "sweaty," meaningful fluid loss still occurs across a
multi-hour meet with repeated warm-up and race swimming. Drinking to thirst
between events, rather than assuming water immersion negates the need to
drink, is the practical guard against this.

## Open-water and long-distance swim events

Marathon/open-water swims lasting an hour or more (10km races, channel
swims) fall outside the short-event pattern above and instead follow
endurance-fueling principles much closer to `marathon-fueling.md`: in-swim
carbohydrate intake (commonly handed to the swimmer at feed stations by a
support crew, since swimmers can't carry gels the way runners or cyclists
can) becomes relevant, and pre-event carbohydrate loading and hydration
planning matter for the same glycogen-management reasons as any other
hour-plus endurance effort.
