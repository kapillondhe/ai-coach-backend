# Macronutrient Basics

## Energy density

Protein and carbohydrates each provide about 4 kcal per gram; fat provides about
9 kcal per gram; alcohol provides about 7 kcal per gram. Total daily calorie need is
the starting point for any macro plan — macros are a way of splitting that total, not
a substitute for getting the total right.

## Setting a macro split

A common general-fitness starting point, once daily calories and protein (see
protein-timing.md) are set, is to split the remaining calories roughly 25-35% from fat
and the rest from carbohydrate. Fat intake below about 20% of total calories risks
insufficient essential fatty acid intake and can impair hormone production; there is
little evidence of extra benefit from pushing fat much above 35% for most goals other
than specific medical or ketogenic-diet contexts.

## Carbohydrate needs scale with training volume

Carbohydrate needs vary more than protein or fat needs depending on training volume:

- Light training (under ~1 hour/day, low intensity): 3-5 g/kg/day.
- Moderate training (1-2 hours/day): 5-7 g/kg/day.
- High-volume endurance training (2+ hours/day): 6-10 g/kg/day.

Under-fueling carbohydrate relative to training volume is a common cause of
persistent fatigue, poor recovery, and stalled performance in athletes who are
otherwise eating "healthy."

## Calorie deficits for fat loss

A moderate deficit of about 300-500 kcal/day below maintenance produces steady fat
loss (roughly 0.25-0.5 kg/week) while minimizing muscle loss and adherence problems
associated with more aggressive deficits. Deficits larger than about 25% of
maintenance calories increase the risk of losing lean muscle mass alongside fat,
especially without adequate protein intake and resistance training.
