# Protein Intake and Timing

## Daily protein targets

Total daily protein intake matters far more than timing it around workouts. Sports-nutrition
guidelines generally recommend the following ranges, in grams of protein per kilogram of body
weight per day:

- Sedentary adults, general health: 0.8-1.0 g/kg/day.
- Recreational exercisers (general fitness): 1.2-1.6 g/kg/day.
- Muscle gain / hypertrophy training: 1.6-2.2 g/kg/day.
- Fat loss (calorie deficit): 1.8-2.4 g/kg/day, higher end of the range to preserve
  lean muscle mass while eating at a deficit.
- Endurance athletes (running, cycling): 1.2-1.6 g/kg/day, to support muscle repair
  and, secondarily, some energy contribution during long efforts.

These ranges assume a person of typical body composition; very lean or very high
body-fat individuals may target protein relative to lean body mass instead of total
body weight.

## Per-meal distribution

Evidence supports spreading protein across 3-5 meals of roughly 0.3-0.4 g/kg each
(about 20-40g for most adults) rather than concentrating it in one meal, to maximize
muscle protein synthesis throughout the day. There is no strong evidence for a narrow
post-workout "anabolic window" — what matters most is hitting the daily total
consistently; consuming protein within a few hours of training is sufficient.

## Protein quality

Complete protein sources (containing all essential amino acids in sufficient amounts)
include meat, fish, eggs, dairy, and soy. Combining incomplete plant sources across
the day (e.g. grains with legumes) achieves the same effect for plant-based eaters.
Leucine content is the key driver of muscle protein synthesis; animal proteins and soy
tend to be higher in leucine per gram than most other plant proteins, which is why
plant-based eaters targeting muscle gain are often advised toward the higher end of the
intake range or to include a leucine-rich source like soy or a fortified pea-protein
blend at each meal.
