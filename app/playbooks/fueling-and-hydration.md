Ask first: session or race length, body weight (only if needed for g/kg), and
what they currently eat or have tried. For daily protein, use the protein
calculator tool.

During exercise (carbohydrate per hour):
- Under about 60-75 min: nothing needed beyond water for most sessions.
- 1-2.5 h: 30-60 g/h.
- Over 2.5 h, and all 70.3 / full-distance racing: 60-90 g/h, using products
  that mix glucose and fructose. Trained athletes can go higher, but only after
  practicing it.
- Most people under-fuel long rides and pay for it on the run. The bike is
  where triathletes eat; the run is for topping up.
- Gut training: practice race fueling in long sessions for 4-8 weeks before the
  race, building up the amount. Never try a new product on race day.

Before:
- Pre-race meal 2-4 h out: mostly carbohydrate, low in fat and fiber, familiar
  food (about 1-4 g/kg depending on how long before).
- Carb loading only for events over about 90 minutes: higher carbs for 1-2 days
  before (about 8-12 g/kg/day) with less fiber. It isn't eating a huge dinner
  the night before.

Hydration and sodium:
- Sweat rates vary a lot (roughly 0.5-2 L/h). Suggest a sweat test: weigh before
  and after a 1-hour session; each kg lost is about 1 L.
- Aim to replace part of the losses, not all: losing up to about 2% of body
  weight is fine. Gaining weight during a race means overdrinking, which risks
  hyponatremia.
- Sodium: individual. Many athletes do well with about 300-600 mg per hour in
  long or hot events; salty sweaters (white residue on kit) may need more.
- Caffeine works: about 3 mg/kg about an hour before, or smaller doses later in
  long races. Practice it first.

Daily eating:
- Match carbs to training: more on big days, less on rest days. Roughly 5-7
  g/kg on a moderate day (about an hour of training), 6-10 g/kg on heavy days.
- Protein: about 1.2-1.6 g/kg/day for endurance training (higher while losing
  weight), spread over 3-5 meals. Use the protein calculator for their number.
- After hard or long sessions: carbs plus about 20-40 g protein within a couple
  of hours. The exact timing matters less than total daily intake, unless
  there's another session the same day.

Weight and under-fueling:
- Avoid big deficits during heavy training or race build. If weight loss is a
  goal, do it in base phase, slowly, and keep fueling the key sessions.
- Red flags of low energy availability: missed or irregular periods, frequent
  illness, stress fractures, persistent fatigue, falling performance despite
  training, obsessive restriction. Recommend seeing a doctor or sports
  dietitian; it isn't a training-load fix.
