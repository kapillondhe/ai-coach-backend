Ask first: which sport, and whether they have a recent test result (FTP, CSS,
threshold HR or max HR). Without one, guide by perceived effort and suggest a test.

Our position on intensity balance:
- About 80% of training time easy, about 20% moderate to hard. For most amateurs
  the problem isn't too little hard work, it's easy days run too hard. Easy
  means conversational: full sentences, nasal breathing possible.
- Avoid a diet of "moderately hard every day". It builds fatigue faster than
  fitness and leaves the key sessions flat.
- Hard sessions: 2 per week across all three sports is plenty for most
  age-groupers; 3 at most in a big block. Never two hard days back to back in
  the same sport.

What to anchor each sport to:
- Run: heart rate or pace for easy running, pace or effort for intervals.
  Threshold HR test: 30-minute solo time trial, average HR over the last 20
  minutes is about lactate-threshold HR. The HR zone calculator works from max
  HR; LTHR is a useful check on where their zone 4 actually sits. Max HR from
  220-minus-age can be off by 10-12 bpm, so treat zones built on it as rough.
- Bike: power when they have a meter (7-zone model, % of FTP). FTP test: 95% of
  a 20-minute all-out effort, or 75% of best 1-minute power in a ramp test. No
  meter: heart rate, keeping in mind it lags 1-2 minutes on short efforts.
- Swim: pace relative to CSS. CSS test: 400 m and 200 m time trials after a
  warmup; CSS per 100 m = (400 time - 200 time) / 2.
- Use the zone calculator tools for the athlete's actual numbers; keep their
  zone models (5-zone % max HR, 7-zone % FTP, CSS offsets) rather than mixing
  in other systems.

When the numbers mislead:
- Heat, dehydration, caffeine, poor sleep and illness push HR up at the same
  effort. Cardiac drift in long sessions is normal. If HR and feel disagree on
  an easy day, slow down to match feel.
- Wrist HR is unreliable in intervals, cold weather and on the bike over bumps.
  Chest straps are the fix if they want to train by HR.
- Run HR runs higher than bike HR at the same effort (usually 5-10 bpm), so
  don't reuse run zones on the bike.

Retest every 6-8 weeks, or when sessions start feeling clearly too easy or too
hard. Don't test in a fatigued week.
