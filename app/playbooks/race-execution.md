title: Taper and race execution
description: Taper, race week, race-day pacing for sprint/Olympic/70.3/full
  distance and running races, transitions (T1/T2), running off the bike, racing
  in the heat, race-day routine, what to do when the race goes wrong, target
  finish times.
---

Ask first: race distance and date, their recent key-session numbers (FTP or
bike HR, run pace, swim pace), and the course (hills, heat, wetsuit or not).

Taper:
- Length: about 7-10 days for Sprint/Olympic, about 2 weeks for 70.3, 2-3 weeks
  for full distance.
- Cut volume by roughly 40-60%, keep a little intensity (short race-pace efforts)
  and keep the usual session frequency. Cutting intensity is what makes people
  feel flat.
- Feeling sluggish or "phantom aches" during taper is normal. Don't add training
  to fix it.
- Race week: nothing new: no new kit, food, or shoes. Last real effort about 3-4
  days out; a short loosener the day before.

Pacing principle: the race is decided late. Start conservatively and aim for
even or slightly negative effort. Going out too hard is the most common and
most costly mistake at every distance.

Bike pacing (% of FTP), lower end for less experienced or slower athletes:
- Sprint: about 90-95%. Olympic: 85-92%. 70.3: 75-83%. Full: 68-78%.
- No power meter: Olympic around threshold-minus effort; 70.3 "steady, could
  talk in short phrases"; full distance "all-day easy-moderate".
- Ride the hills at steady effort, not steady speed. Don't spike power on climbs.

Swim: settle in after the first 200-300 m. Starting slightly back or wide is
worth more than a fast first minute.

Run off the bike: the first 1-2 km always feels awkward. Run them deliberately
easier than goal pace. In 70.3 and full distance, walking aid stations from the
start is a valid strategy, not a failure.

Transitions: lay out kit in the order you'll use it and practice it a couple of
times. Free time here costs nothing in fitness.

Heat:
- Slow down early, by feel; heart rate will be higher at the same pace.
- Cool with ice and water on the body, and drink to plan.
- If they can, do 7-14 days of heat acclimation before a hot race (training in
  heat or hot-bath protocols).
- Confusion, stopping sweating, or collapse is a medical emergency.

When it goes wrong:
- Cramping or gut trouble: slow down, take a few minutes easier, get fuel and
  fluid back in.
- Blew up: walk-run to the finish, or stop. Stopping is the right call with any
  red-flag symptom (chest pain, dizziness that doesn't settle, confusion).

Running races (marathon, half): same principles. Even or negative splits, the
first third should feel too easy, adjust goal pace for heat and hills.
