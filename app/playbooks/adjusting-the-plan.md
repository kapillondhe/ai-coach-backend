title: Adjusting when life gets in the way
description: Missed sessions or a missed week, being sick or coming back from
  illness, travel, busy work weeks, stress, feeling unmotivated, a bad session,
  whether to make up workouts, moving sessions around, changing or dropping a
  race goal.
---

Ask first: what was missed and why (busy, sick, injured, tired), how long, and
how far out the race is.

Missed sessions:
- Don't make up missed workouts by stacking them into later days. Skip it and
  carry on with the plan.
- If a key session was missed, move it only if there's room without putting two
  hard days together; otherwise drop an easy session that week instead.
- Rough guide by time off (not sick or injured):
  - 1-3 days: carry on as planned.
  - 4-7 days: two or three easy days, then resume the plan.
  - 1-2 weeks: repeat the previous week's volume before moving on.
  - More than 2 weeks: rebuild over about the same length of time as the break,
    and check whether the race goal still fits.

Illness:
- Fever, chest symptoms, body aches or stomach illness: no training. Rest until
  symptoms are gone and they've had a normal day, then start with short easy
  sessions.
- Only head-cold symptoms (runny nose, sneezing, mild sore throat) and no fever:
  easy training can be fine. If they feel worse after 10 minutes, stop.
- After a fever or a significant viral illness, take returning slowly: easy only
  for a few days. Chest pain, palpitations, or unusual breathlessness during the
  return means stop and see a doctor before training again.
- Don't try to "sweat it out".

Busy weeks, travel and stress:
- Life stress counts as training stress. A hard week at work is a good week to
  cut volume, not to push through the full plan.
- Minimum to keep fitness: two short quality sessions plus whatever easy time
  fits. Fitness drops far more slowly than people fear; a week or two of reduced
  training costs very little.
- Travel: bodyweight strength, hotel gym, or easy runs are enough. Long-haul
  flights plus a hard session the next day is a bad combination.

Bad sessions and low motivation:
- One bad session means nothing; a run of them (3+ in a row, or a week of feeling
  flat) is a fatigue signal. Treat it as one (see recovery-and-readiness) rather
  than training harder.
- Low motivation for weeks plus poor sleep or mood changes can be overreaching
  or something outside training; ease off and, if it persists, suggest talking to
  a doctor.

Changing the goal: if the race is close and too much was lost, offer real
options: race it as a training day, go down a distance, or move to a later
race. Health and the next season matter more than one event.
