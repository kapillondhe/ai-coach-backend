Ask first: training phase and race date, gym access or home only, and any
current pain.

Our position: strength training is worth doing for almost every endurance
athlete. It improves economy (less energy at the same pace) and lowers injury
risk, and it doesn't cause unwanted bulk at endurance volumes.

The minimum that works:
- Twice a week, 20-40 minutes, in base and build phases. Once a week keeps the
  gains in peak phase.
- Mostly heavier loads with low reps (3-6 reps, 3-4 sets, stopping 1-2 reps
  short of failure) once technique is solid. Light high-rep circuits mostly add
  more endurance work.
- Main patterns: squat or leg press, hip hinge (deadlift, RDL), single-leg work
  (split squat, step-up), calf raises (straight and bent knee, heavy), a pull
  (rows, pull-ups) and a push. Core: anti-rotation and bracing (planks, Pallof
  press, dead bugs) rather than endless crunches.
- Runners: add plyometrics once strength is there: skips, pogo hops, bounds,
  low volume, twice a week.
- Beginners: start with bodyweight or light loads for 2-4 weeks to learn
  movements, then progress load.

Fitting it in:
- Put strength on a hard day (after the key session, or later the same day), or
  on a day without a key session the next morning. Avoid heavy legs the day
  before a key bike or run session.
- Expect some extra soreness in the first 2-3 weeks of a block, then it fades.
- Last heavy session about 7-10 days before a goal race. Race week: nothing new,
  light activation only.

Mobility and stretching:
- Before training: a short dynamic warmup (leg swings, lunges, skips, easy
  build-ups) instead of long static stretches.
- Static stretching after training or in the evening is fine if they like it,
  but it doesn't prevent injuries on its own. Target what limits them (hip
  flexors for bike position, ankles for running, thoracic spine and shoulders
  for swimming).
- Mobility shouldn't replace strength work; strength through range does more.
