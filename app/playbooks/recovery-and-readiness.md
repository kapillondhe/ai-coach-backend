Ask first: how long they've felt this way, what their training and sleep have
looked like over the last 2-3 weeks, and whether anything else is going on
(illness, work stress). If their synced data has HRV, resting HR or sleep,
look at the trend.

Reading fatigue:
- Normal: tired legs the day after a hard session, heavier legs in a big week.
  That's training working, and it clears with an easy day or two.
- Overreaching (needs a few easy days to a week): performance down, HR not
  rising normally in efforts, poor sleep, irritability, lingering soreness, for
  1-2 weeks.
- Possible overtraining or something medical (needs time off and a doctor):
  performance down and fatigue present for weeks despite rest, plus mood
  changes, frequent illness, or unexplained weight change. Rule out medical
  causes (low iron, thyroid, under-fueling, post-viral) before blaming training.
- Our default: when in doubt, take 2-3 genuinely easy days and reassess. That
  costs little; pushing through real fatigue costs weeks.

Wearable data (HRV, resting HR, readiness scores):
- Look at trends, not single days. One low HRV morning after a late night is
  noise. A 7-day average sitting clearly below their normal range, or resting
  HR several beats above normal for 3+ days, is a signal.
- Combine data with how they feel. Low numbers but feeling good: train, but keep
  it controlled. Good numbers but feeling awful: listen to the body.
- Readiness scores are each brand's own estimate. Useful as a prompt to check
  in, not as a rule.

Sleep is the biggest recovery tool: aim for 7-9 hours, more in heavy weeks.
A regular schedule and limiting alcohol close to bed matter more than gadgets.
Short naps (20-30 min) help when night sleep is short.

Recovery structure:
- At least one rest or very easy day per week.
- Recovery week every 3rd or 4th week: about 30-50% less volume, a little
  intensity kept so they don't feel flat.
- Easy days must actually be easy (see intensity-and-zones).

Recovery tools (massage, compression, foam rolling, cold water): fine if they
enjoy them, but small effects compared with sleep, food and easy days. Regular
ice baths straight after strength sessions can blunt some strength gains; avoid
that pairing in a strength block.

Muscle soreness (DOMS) peaks at 24-72 h and isn't a reason to stop training;
easy movement helps. Pain that's sharp, in one spot, or changes how they move
is different: see pain-and-injury.
