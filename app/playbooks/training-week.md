Ask first: goal race (distance and date), hours per week they can reliably
train, current volume in each sport, and their weakest sport.

Realistic weekly hours (peak weeks; most weeks are lower):
- Sprint: 4-6 h. Olympic: 6-9 h. 70.3: 8-12 h. Full distance: 10-16 h.
- If they have less than this, say what's still achievable (finishing well)
  rather than pretending the time doesn't matter.

Time windows from a reasonable base: Sprint 8-12 weeks, Olympic 12-16,
70.3 16-20, full 24-30. If they're starting from little, add base time first.

Shaping the week:
- Pick 2-3 key sessions (one hard bike, one hard run or a long run, a long ride)
  and build the rest of the week around protecting them. Everything else is
  easy.
- Typical split by time: about 15-20% swim, 45-55% bike, 25-35% run. Shift
  time toward the weakest sport, but the bike is the biggest share of race time
  and pays off the most.
- Frequency beats single long sessions, especially for swimming: three 30-45
  minute swims beat one 90-minute swim.
- A rest or very easy day each week. One long day is fine if the next day is easy.
- Time-crunched (6-8 h): keep the long ride and long run, make most other
  sessions short, and use indoor bike sessions for quality.

Phases: base (aerobic volume, technique, strength), build (race-specific
intensity, bricks, longer key sessions), peak (race simulation), taper.
Recovery week every 3rd or 4th week: about 30-50% less volume, keep a little
intensity.

Progression:
- Increase total weekly volume gradually, roughly 5-10% per week in build
  weeks, and treat running more carefully than bike or swim. Big jumps in run
  volume (more than about 30% over a couple of weeks) are linked to injury.
- Add duration before intensity. Don't raise volume and intensity in the same week.
- Long run caps: most 70.3 athletes don't need more than about 1h45-2h; full
  distance about 2h30-2h45. Longer runs cost more recovery than they return.

Bricks: a short run straight off the bike (10-30 minutes) once a week in build
phase is enough to teach the legs; long bricks are for race simulation only,
a few times per build.

Single-sport athletes (marathon, cycling, swim): same principles. 3-6 sessions
in the sport, one long session, one or two quality sessions, the rest easy.
