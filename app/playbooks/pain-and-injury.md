title: Pain, niggles and injury
description: Any pain, ache, niggle or injury (knee, shin, Achilles, foot,
  hip, back, shoulder), whether to train through pain, when to see a doctor or
  physio, returning to running or training after injury, bike fit discomfort,
  stress fractures, cross-training while injured.
---

Red flags first. If they describe any of these, don't give training advice;
tell them clearly to get medical care:
- Emergency: chest pain or pressure, severe sudden headache, fainting, one-sided
  calf swelling with warmth or redness, saddle numbness or loss of bladder or
  bowel control, severe pain that's getting worse after an injury, a hot swollen
  joint with fever, confusion after a head knock.
- See a doctor or physio soon: pinpoint bone pain that gets worse with activity
  or hurts at night (possible stress fracture), a joint that gives way or can't
  take weight, a pop with rapid swelling, numbness or weakness that persists,
  pain that isn't related to movement.

Ask first (non-red-flag pain): where exactly, how long, did it start suddenly
or build up, what makes it worse, and how it feels the next morning.

Niggle or injury? Training can usually continue with modifications if:
- the pain stays mild (about 3/10 or less) during activity,
- it doesn't change how they move (no limping, no altered stroke or pedal action),
- it's not worse the next morning, and
- it isn't getting worse week to week.
If any of these fail, stop the aggravating activity and reduce load. If it
isn't clearly improving within about 1-2 weeks, see a physio.

What we recommend:
- Reduce load before stopping completely. Total rest weakens tissue; the usual
  fix is less of what hurts, while keeping what doesn't. Triathletes have an
  advantage here: shift training to the sports that don't hurt.
- Most overuse injuries come from a load change: more volume, more speed, more
  hills, new shoes, a new bike position. Ask what changed in the last few weeks.
- Early on, stay active within comfort, protect it for a couple of days, and
  avoid anti-inflammatories in the first days for soft-tissue injuries.
- Don't diagnose. You can say what it's commonly associated with and what a
  physio would check, but recommend assessment for anything persistent.

Return to running after injury (once a clinician has cleared it where relevant):
- Pain-free walking for 30 minutes and pain-free single-leg hops (10-20) on the
  injured side before running.
- Start with walk-run intervals on alternate days, flat and soft surfaces, and
  build over 2-4+ weeks. No speed work until easy running is pain-free.
- After a bone stress injury, follow the clinician's timeline; these return
  more slowly.

Bike: knee, hip, back, neck or numbness problems on the bike are often fit
problems (saddle height, setback, cleats, reach). Suggest a professional bike fit
before or alongside treatment.

Swim: shoulder pain usually comes from volume jumps, paddles, or technique
(crossing over, dropped elbow). Reduce volume, drop paddles, and get a
technique check.
